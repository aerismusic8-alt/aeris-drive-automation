## 2. Operating Principle

The Master Blueprint is the canonical product-production source.

AX does not require every AI to use the same execution method. AX adapts the blueprint to the verified capabilities of each available AI.

An AI is an optional executor, not a dependency of AX.

If an AI can be reached and produces a verifiable product, retain it as an executor candidate.
If it cannot be reached or repeatedly fails practical execution, skip it and continue.

- Output quality

- Failed task types

I. Mixed / General Production

## 4. Blueprint Variant Rules

### Coding-capable AI
Blueprint emphasizes executable product, source code, installation/use instructions, tests, packaging, and marketplace listing.

### Research-capable AI
Blueprint emphasizes information product, validated sources, structured findings, decision-ready report, and commercial packaging.

### Visual-capable AI
Blueprint emphasizes visual product, reusable assets, commercial formats, thumbnails/mockups, and marketplace-ready previews.

### Marketing-capable AI
Blueprint emphasizes offer positioning, sales copy, product listing, promotional content, and channel-specific variants.

### High-speed/general AI
Blueprint emphasizes short production cycles, repeatable product templates, volume production, and lightweight QC.

### High-quality/slow AI
Blueprint emphasizes higher-value products, deeper research/design, stronger QC, and premium positioning.

## 6. Production Priority

Priority order:

1. Fastest credible path to market
2. Lowest additional cost
3. Clear customer value
4. Reusable production pattern
5. Multiple distribution channels
6. Evidence-based improvement

The system should favor products that can be created and released quickly, then use real market feedback to improve the next iteration.

## 7. Multi-Channel Release

For every commercially viable product, evaluate applicable channels such as marketplace, digital product store, social media, YouTube/Shorts, direct sales, affiliate/referral channel, and community/channel distribution.

Do not require every product to use every channel. Select channels based on product fit and time-to-market.

## 8. Versioning

Master Blueprint version format: vMAJOR.MINOR

Examples:
- v1.0 = initial canonical blueprint
- v1.1 = improved process/capability mapping
- v1.2 = improved market/QC rules
- v2.0 = major structural change

Every variant inherits the current Master Blueprint version and adds an executor-specific suffix.

Examples:
- AX-MPB-v1.0
- AX-MPB-v1.0-GEMINI-CODE
- AX-MPB-v1.0-GEMINI-VISUAL
- AX-MPB-v1.0-CODEX-SOFTWARE

## 9. Learning Loop

After each production cycle AX records:

- What was attempted
- Which executor was used
- What was produced
- Time consumed
- Verification result
- Market response
- Failure cause
- Improvement

Successful improvements are promoted into the Master Blueprint.
Executor-specific improvements remain in the corresponding variant unless proven generally useful.

## 10. Decision Rules

RULE 1 — Product first.
Do not add infrastructure merely because it is interesting.

RULE 2 — Execution over explanation.
A usable artifact is more valuable than a long plan.

RULE 3 — AI optionality.
Use external AI when it increases throughput or quality; never make AX dependent on it.

RULE 4 — Verify reality.
A claimed completion is not enough. Verify the actual artifact/output.

RULE 5 — Fail fast, learn, move on.
If an executor cannot perform the practical task, record the failure and continue.

RULE 6 — Reuse successful patterns.
A successful blueprint variant becomes a reusable production template.

RULE 7 — Market feedback controls iteration.
Real customer response has higher priority than theoretical optimization.

## 11. First Production Target

Use this blueprint immediately to generate products that are inexpensive or free to produce, deliverable digitally, publishable quickly, capable of multiple-channel distribution, and measurable by real market response.
